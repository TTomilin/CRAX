Abstract

Safety is a core concern for deploying reinforcement learning (RL) agents in real-world domains such as robotics and autonomous driving. While benchmarks have been central to progress in RL, existing 3D physics-based safety benchmarks remain computationally slow, limiting large-scale experimentation and rapid prototyping. To address this gap, we propose CRAX (Constrained RL Accelerated with JAX). Built on top of the MuJoCo XLA (MJX) physics engine, CRAX leverages vectorized operations and hardware acceleration, yielding up to 200x faster training over comparable CPU-based safety benchmarks. The benchmark features eight tasks spanning three difficulty levels and multiple agent morphologies. Evaluating seven popular safe RL methods, we find that none dominates across tasks, and that learning safe policies from pixels remains largely unsolved.

Three difficulty levels

Each suite ramps from a lenient setting most methods can solve to one that leaves ample room for improvement: more varied hazards, smaller goals, moving targets, or stricter bounds.

Learning from pixels

Every task exposes an egocentric camera mounted on the agent and an allocentric camera observing it from outside. Frames are rendered on the GPU inside the same compiled loop as the simulation, so pixel observations never leave the device. Policies trained from pixels receive these views at 64×64.

Algorithms

All baselines share one fully compiled JAX training loop with hooks for custom losses and constraint handling, so adding a method means writing its loss, not its infrastructure. Select one with --alg.

Results

Two orders of magnitude faster

On identical hardware, CRAX runs 25–180× faster than the peak of Safety-Gymnasium. Safety-Gymnasium saturates at 3K–20K steps per second and runs out of CPU memory at a few hundrer parallel environments. CRAX keeps scaling to thousands of parallel environments and peaks at roughly 1M SPS on Point Goal and Ant Velocity and 340K SPS on Humanoid Velocity.

The full CRAX evaluation suite (algorithms × environments × levels × seeds) is hundreds of runs and trillions of environment steps. CRAX completes it in 2 weeks on a single H100. The same study on Safety-Gymnasium would take close to a year!

Ramp up the seeds

Few seeds give noisy results, and many seeds used to be too expensive. On Humanoid Height, three seeds leave the methods' confidence bands overlapping. CRAX runs twenty seeds in about six hours, enough for a clear ranking.

How many seeds are enough?

CRAX's throughput lets us achieve reliable results. We run 20 seeds on every task and measure how the 95% confidence interval narrows as seeds are added. Relative to the mean, it shrinks from about 90% at two seeds to 40% at three and 22% at five, after which gains are small.

The curves also show that some tasks are far more volatile than others. Point Goal and Point Circle are tight from the start, while Spider Lift and Humanoid Velocity stay wide well past three seeds. A seed count that suffices for one task can mislead on another. 10 seeds keep the interval within 30% of the mean on every task.

Evaluation on Level 1

Seven SafeRL baselines plus unconstrained PPO, trained for 500M steps with 10 seeds on every Level 1 task. The dashed line marks the cost threshold d = 25. Methods strike different trade-offs between performance and safety, and none is best everywhere.

PPO sets an example of obtainable reward when safety is ignored. The Lagrangian methods keep cost close to the threshold: PPOPID stays within it on every task, PPOLag on all but one. PPOCost settles for a compromise with no guarantee of satisfying the constraint, while PPOSauté is safe on Reacher, Height and Pathway but otherwise behaves much like PPO. FOCOPS is strongest on navigation (Goal, Push) and Reacher, yet weakest on forward locomotion (Lift, Height, Pathway).

Vision makes learning a safe policy harder

Every method earns less reward from egocentric frames than from state vectors, and less still from allocentric frames. No method learns to push the block from allocentric pixels, and on the remaining five environments none learns a meaningful policy from pixels at all.

Hmm, where could the goal be?

Varying safety requirements

Real applications differ in how much risk they tolerate, so CRAX allows the cost bound to be adjustable. We explore the effects of this by training PPOLag on Level 1 with bounds of 15, 25 (the default) and 35.

One full Level 1 episode (1000 steps) of PPOLag per bound. Both collect almost the same reward. Only the risk they take differs.

PPOLag tracks each bound closely. Its cost settles on the dotted line every time. The trade-off is not symmetric: tightening the bound from 25 to 15 costs more reward than loosening it to 35 gains, most visibly on Point Push and Reacher Reach.

Curriculum learning & safety transfer

Can easier levels or an unconstrained warm start help on the hardest tasks? We compare three regimes under the same budget of 500M steps: Normal trains on Level 3 from scratch for all 500M steps. Curriculum steps through Levels 1, 2 and 3 with 167M steps each, carrying parameters over. Transfer trains an unconstrained PPO policy on Level 3 for 250M steps, then fine-tunes it with a safe RL method for the remaining 250M.

Neither curriculum learning nor safety transfer helps everywhere. On Walker Pathway, both raise the reward of PPOLag, PPOPID and P3O while staying under the bound, since direct training leaves much of the safety budget unused. On Reacher Reach, curriculum lifts PPOLag and P3O, but transfer breaks safety for every method except FOCOPS. On Point Goal, direct training remains best.

Get started

Install

git clone https://github.com/TTomilin/CRAX.git
cd CRAX
pip install -e ".[train,cuda]"

Train

python -m training.train_env \
  --env_name safe_goal_point --difficulty 1 \
  --alg ppo_lag

Play with the keyboard

pip install pygame
python -m crax.play --env_name safe_push_point --level 3

Step an environment

import jax
from crax import envs

env = envs.get_environment('safe_goal_point', level=1)
reset_fn, step_fn = jax.jit(env.reset), jax.jit(env.step)

rng = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)
state = reset_fn(rng)
for _ in range(300):
    rng, key = jax.random.split(rng)
    action = jax.random.uniform(key, (env.action_size,),
                                minval=-1.0, maxval=1.0)
    state = step_fn(state, action)
    reward = float(state.reward)
    cost = float(state.info['cost'])  # safety cost per step
